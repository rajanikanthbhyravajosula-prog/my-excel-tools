## Part A: What Has Changed Since 1994
| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| **CPC Amendment Acts of 1999 and 2002** (in force from 1 July 2002) | Written statement due within **30 days**, extendable to a maximum of **90 days** (Order VIII Rule 1). No more than **3 adjournments** per party (Order XVII Rule 1). |
| **Section 89** added | Courts must refer suitable cases to ADR: arbitration, conciliation, mediation, Lok Adalat. |
| **Commercial Courts Act, 2015** | For commercial disputes of ₹3 lakh and above, the 120-day limit for the written statement is strict. Also brings pre-institution mediation (S.12A) and case management hearings. |
| **New criminal laws, from 1 July 2024** | IPC, CrPC and the Evidence Act are replaced by the **BNS, BNSS and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam**. Evidence rules apply in civil trials too. |
| **E-filing and virtual hearings** | Now routine in most High Courts and many district courts. |

## Part B: Structure of the CPC
1. **Sections 1–158** contain the substantive principles, such as jurisdiction, res judicata, appeals and execution. Only **Parliament** can amend them.
2. **The First Schedule, with 51 Orders**, contains the procedural rules. **High Courts** can amend these for their own state (Section 122). Andhra Pradesh has its own local amendments.

> **Memory aid:** Sections tell you *what* the law is. Orders tell you *how* to do it.
> Where a Section and a Rule conflict, the **Section prevails**.

## Part C: Key Definitions (Section 2)
### 1. Decree, S.2(2)
The formal expression of an adjudication that **conclusively determines the rights of the parties** on all or any of the matters in controversy in the suit.

**Five essentials:**
1. There is an adjudication (a judicial decision).
2. It is made in a **suit**.
3. It determines the **rights of the parties**.
4. The determination is **conclusive**, meaning final as far as that court is concerned.
5. It is **formally expressed**.

**Three kinds:**
- **Preliminary decree:** rights are decided but further proceedings are needed, e.g. partition suits and suits for accounts.
- **Final decree:** the suit is completely disposed of.
- **Partly preliminary, partly final:** e.g. a decree for possession (final) together with an inquiry into mesne profits (preliminary).

**Deemed decrees:** rejection of a plaint (Order VII Rule 11), and determination of a question under Section 144 (restitution).

**Not a decree:** an order from which an appeal lies as from an order, and dismissal for default.

### Clarification: "an order from which an appeal lies as from an order"
**Where it is:** CPC **S.2(2)**, the exclusion clause. A decree "shall not include **(a)** any adjudication from which an appeal lies as an appeal from an order, or **(b)** any order of dismissal for default."

**Meaning:** some decisions look final, but the CPC gives them their own appeal route as *orders*. Because they have that separate route, they are **not decrees**, even if they end the matter.

| | Appeal from a decree | Appeal from an order |
|---|---|---|
| Provision | **S.96** (first appeal), **S.100** (second appeal) | **S.104** and **O.43 R.1** (a closed list) |
| Second appeal? | Yes, on a substantial question of law (**S.100**) | **No**: **S.104(2)** bars a second appeal from an appellate order |
| Time limit | Limitation Act **Art.116**: 90 days to the High Court, 30 days to any other court | Same, **Art.116** |

**Common appealable orders under O.43 R.1:**
- **(a)** returning a plaint under **O.7 R.10** (wrong court)
- **(c)** rejecting an application under **O.9 R.9** to restore a suit dismissed for default
- **(d)** rejecting an application under **O.9 R.13** to set aside an ex parte decree
- **(r)** granting or refusing a temporary injunction under **O.39 R.1, R.2, R.2A, R.4, R.10**
- **(s)** appointing a receiver under **O.40 R.1, R.4**

**Orders not in that list** cannot be appealed separately. Under **S.105**, an error in such an order can be raised in the appeal against the final decree.

| Decision | Status | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Rejection** of plaint (**O.7 R.11**) | **Deemed decree** (included in S.2(2)) | First appeal under **S.96** |
| **Return** of plaint (**O.7 R.10**) | **Appealable order** (excluded from S.2(2)) | Appeal under **O.43 R.1(a)** |
| Dismissal for default (**O.9 R.8**) | Neither a decree nor an appealable order | Restoration application under **O.9 R.9**. If that is rejected, appeal under **O.43 R.1(c)** |

> **Trick:** "If the CPC gives it a ticket in **O.43**, it travels as an **order**, never as a decree." Rejection = decree. Return = order.

### 2. Judgment, Order and the parties
| Term | Section | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Judgment | S.2(9) | The statement the judge gives of the **grounds** of a decree or order |
| Order | S.2(14) | The formal expression of any decision of a civil court that is **not a decree** |
| Decree-holder | S.2(3) | The person in whose favour a decree is passed or an executable order made |
| Judgment-debtor | S.2(10) | The person against whom it is passed |
| Legal Representative | S.2(11) | A person who in law represents the estate of a deceased person, including an intermeddler |
| Mesne Profits | S.2(12) | Profits a person in **wrongful possession** actually received or might with ordinary diligence have received, **plus interest**. Profits from improvements made by that person are excluded. |

> **Judgment** = the reasons. **Decree** = the final operative result. **Order** = any other decision.

## Part D: Section 9, Jurisdiction of Civil Courts
> *"The Courts shall have jurisdiction to try all suits of a civil nature excepting suits of which their cognizance is either expressly or impliedly barred."*

### Suit of a civil nature
The main question in the suit concerns a **civil right**, such as property, contract or office.
- **Explanation I:** a suit about a right to property or to an office is civil even if it depends on religious rites or ceremonies.
- **Explanation II:** this applies whether or not any fees are attached to the office.
- A right to worship in a temple is civil in nature. A suit purely to uphold caste dignity or social status is not.

### Express and implied bars
- **Express bar:** the statute itself excludes the civil court, e.g. **Section 34 of the SARFAESI Act** (matters before the DRT), the Income-tax Act, and certain land revenue and tenancy laws.
- **Implied bar:** the statute creates a special right together with a special remedy and tribunal, so ouster of the civil court is inferred.

### Landmark cases
| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| *Dhulabhai v. State of M.P.* (1968, SC) | Exclusion of civil court jurisdiction **must not be readily inferred**. Even where it is barred, the civil court can examine cases where the tribunal acted **without jurisdiction** or ignored fundamental principles of judicial procedure. |
| *Kiran Singh v. Chaman Paswan* (1954, SC) | A decree passed without **inherent** jurisdiction is a **nullity** and can be challenged at any stage, even in execution. |

> **Golden rule:** there is always a presumption **in favour** of civil court jurisdiction.
> **Trick:** "The civil court is the default doctor. A specialist (tribunal) takes over only if the law clearly says so."
