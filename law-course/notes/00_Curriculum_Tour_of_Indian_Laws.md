# A Tour of Indian Law and the Full Course Curriculum
_Law Refresher Course for a 1994 LLB graduate returning to practice  |  Updated October 2026_

## Part 1: How Indian Law Is Organised

### 1.1 The Constitution sits at the top
Every law in India draws its authority from the **Constitution of India (1950)**. A law that conflicts with it is void (Article 13). The Constitution:
- creates the three organs: **Legislature** (makes law), **Executive** (enforces law) and **Judiciary** (interprets law)
- divides law-making power between the Centre and the States through the **Seventh Schedule**:
  - **Union List**: Parliament alone, e.g. banking, securities, income tax
  - **State List**: State Legislature alone, e.g. land, police, local government
  - **Concurrent List**: both, e.g. contracts, civil and criminal procedure, marriage, transfer of property. If they conflict, the central law usually prevails (Article 254).

### 1.2 Sources of law
| Source | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Constitution | The supreme law | Article 21, right to life and personal liberty |
| Statutes (Acts) | Laws made by Parliament or a State Legislature | CPC 1908, Contract Act 1872 |
| Delegated legislation | Rules, regulations and notifications made under an Act | SEBI Regulations, CPC Orders as amended by the High Court |
| Judicial precedent | Decisions of higher courts bind lower courts | Supreme Court law binds all courts (Article 141) |
| Custom and personal law | Customs and religious laws in family matters | Hindu and Muslim personal law |

### 1.3 Hierarchy of courts (Andhra Pradesh example)
| Level | Court | What it hears |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Supreme Court of India**, New Delhi | Final appeals, constitutional matters, Article 32 writs |
| 2 | **High Court of Andhra Pradesh**, Amaravati | Appeals, revisions, Article 226 and 227 writs |
| 3 | **District and Sessions Court** (e.g. at Nandyal) | District-level civil appeals and suits, sessions trials |
| 4 | **Senior Civil Judge** and **Chief Judicial Magistrate** | Civil suits above the junior limit, criminal trials |
| 5 | **Junior Civil Judge** and **Judicial Magistrate** | Smaller civil suits, criminal trials |
| Special | Tribunals and Commissions | Consumer Commissions, DRT, NCLT, Family Courts, MACT, RERA |

> **Memory trick:** "Constitution is the roof, statutes are the walls, rules are the bricks, and judgments are the cement that holds them together."

## Part 2: The Major Changes Since 1994

These are the changes you most need to catch up on, because they did not exist when you graduated.

| Year | Change | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1996 | **Arbitration and Conciliation Act** replaced the 1940 Act | Arbitration is now the main route for commercial disputes |
| 1999 / 2002 | **CPC amendments** | Strict timelines, limited adjournments, ADR under Section 89 |
| 2000 | **Information Technology Act** | Electronic records, e-signatures, cyber offences |
| 2002 | **SARFAESI Act** | Banks recover secured loans without going to court |
| 2005 | **RTI Act**; **Domestic Violence Act**; **Hindu Succession amendment** | Daughters became coparceners (equal birthright in joint family property) |
| 2013 | **Companies Act** and the new **Land Acquisition Act** (RFCTLARR) | Both replaced Acts from the 1950s and 1890s |
| 2015 | **Commercial Courts Act** | Fast-track commercial suits with a strict 120-day written statement |
| 2016 | **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code**; **RERA** | A new insolvency regime; protection for home buyers |
| 2017 | **GST** | One national indirect tax replaced many older taxes |
| 2018 | **Specific Relief Act amendment** | Specific performance is now the normal remedy, not discretionary |
| 2019 | **Consumer Protection Act** | E-commerce and product liability; e-filing of complaints |
| 2023 | **Mediation Act**; **Digital Personal Data Protection Act** | A formal mediation framework; data privacy law |
| 1 July 2024 | **BNS, BNSS and BSA** replaced the **IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act** | All criminal and evidence work now uses the new section numbers |
| 21 Nov 2025 | **Four Labour Codes** in force | They consolidate 29 central labour laws |
| 1 April 2026 | **Income-tax Act, 2025** in force | Replaces the Income-tax Act, 1961 |

> **Verify before use:** laws keep changing. Before you rely on a section in court, check the current bare Act on India Code (indiacode.nic.in) and recent judgments on SCC Online or Indian Kanoon.

## Part 5: How Each Class Works
1. **Lesson**: the important sections explained simply, with examples from your own business.
2. **Cases**: classic landmark judgments plus recent Supreme Court rulings.
3. **Tricks**: mnemonics and comparison tables to help you remember.
4. **Test**: one-line answers, short notes and a practical problem. You reply and I grade.
5. **PDF**: a downloadable copy of every class, so you can build your own notes file.

## Part 6: Steps to Start Practising
- **Enrolment:** apply to the **Bar Council of Andhra Pradesh** with your LLB degree, marks memos, transfer and migration certificates, and the required declarations. A person who has been in other employment declares that they have left it.
- **AIBE:** after enrolment, pass the **All India Bar Examination** to receive the Certificate of Practice. It is an open-book, multiple-choice exam.
- **Junior years:** work under a senior advocate at the Nandyal courts for at least 1 to 2 years. Drafting and court craft are learned by doing.
- **Your advantage:** your 26 years in business, management and FMCG, plus your understanding of markets, give you a natural fit for **commercial, consumer, property and securities** work.

> Check the latest enrolment fees and the AIBE schedule on the official Bar Council of India and Bar Council of Andhra Pradesh websites before you apply.
