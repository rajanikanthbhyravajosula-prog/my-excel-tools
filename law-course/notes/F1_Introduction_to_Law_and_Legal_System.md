# Class F1: Introduction to Law and the Indian Legal System
_Phase 0: Foundations  |  Law Refresher Course  |  Revised with section references_

> **What you will learn today:** what law is, the types of law, where law comes from, how a law is made, how the courts are arranged, the everyday legal words, and how to read a bare Act and a case citation.
> **How to read the references:** "Art." = Article of the Constitution. "S." = Section. "O.7 R.11" = Order 7 Rule 11 of the CPC. "(old ...)" = the section number in the law that was replaced, e.g. "BNS S.303 (old IPC S.378)".

**Abbreviations used:** CPC = Code of Civil Procedure, 1908; GCA = General Clauses Act, 1897; BNS = Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023; BNSS = Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023; BSA = Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023; SRA = Specific Relief Act, 1963; NI Act = Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881; CPA = Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

## 1. What Is Law?
In simple words, **law is a set of rules made or recognised by the State, which everyone must follow, and which the State enforces through courts and penalties.**

### Where "law" is defined
| Provision | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Constitution, Art. 13(3)(a)** | "Law" **includes** any Ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having the force of law in India. |
| **Constitution, Art. 13(3)(b)** | "Laws in force" includes laws passed before the Constitution and not repealed (that is why the Contract Act of 1872 still applies). |
| **Constitution, Art. 13(1) and (2)** | Any law inconsistent with the **fundamental rights** is **void** to that extent. |

### Two classic definitions (from jurisprudence, not from any Act)
| Jurist | Definition | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| **John Austin** | "Law is the **command of the sovereign**, backed by a **sanction**." | Law = an order from the ruler + a punishment for disobeying |
| **John Salmond** | "Law is the body of principles **recognised and applied by the State in the administration of justice**." | Law = what the courts actually apply |

### Law, morality and custom
| | Law | Morality | Custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made by | The State | Society and conscience | Long practice of a community |
| Enforced by | Courts, police, penalties | Shame, guilt | Social pressure |
| Example | Not paying tax is punishable | Lying to a friend is wrong | Giving gifts at weddings |

> **Custom as law:** a custom is recognised as law (Art. 13(3)(a)) only if it is **ancient, certain, reasonable and not against public policy**. These conditions are written into **S.3(a) of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955**, which defines "custom" and "usage".

## 2. Types (Classification) of Law
### A. Civil law vs Criminal law
| Point | Civil law | Criminal law |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Settle disputes; give a **remedy** | **Punish** wrongs against society |
| How a case starts | A **plaint**: CPC **S.26** ("every suit shall be instituted by the presentation of a plaint") and **O.7** | An **FIR**: BNSS **S.173** (old CrPC S.154); or a **complaint** to a Magistrate: BNSS **S.2(1)(h)** (definition) and **S.223** (old CrPC S.200) |
| Who starts it | The injured person (**plaintiff** or **petitioner**) | The **State** (police and the Public Prosecutor), or a **complainant** |
| The other side | **Defendant** or **respondent** | **Accused** |
| Case name | *Ramesh v. Suresh* | *State of Andhra Pradesh v. Suresh* |
| Standard of proof | **Preponderance of probabilities** | **Beyond reasonable doubt** |
| Burden of proof | BSA **S.104** (old Evidence Act S.101): whoever asserts a fact must prove it | Same section, plus the presumption of innocence |
| Meaning of "proved" | BSA **S.2(1)(j)** (old Evidence Act S.3) | Same |
| Result | **Decree**: CPC **S.2(2)**; plus a **judgment**: CPC **S.2(9)** | Conviction and sentence (BNS **S.4** lists the kinds of punishment, old IPC S.53), or acquittal |
| Main laws | CPC, Contract Act, Transfer of Property Act | BNS, BNSS, BSA |

> **Trick:** "Civil = **compensation**; Criminal = **punishment**." Civil is about *my right*; criminal is about *society's peace*.

### B. Substantive law vs Procedural law
| Substantive law | Procedural law |
|---|---|
| Creates and defines **rights and duties** | Lays down the **method** to enforce those rights |
| *What* the law is | *How* to go to court |
| Contract Act (e.g. **S.2(h)** defines "contract"); BNS (e.g. **S.303** defines theft) | CPC, BNSS, Limitation Act |

> **Trick:** substantive law is the **destination**; procedural law is the **road map**.

### C. Other classifications
| Type | Meaning | Example and section |
|---|---|---|
| **Public law** | Between the State and citizens | Constitution; BNS; tax laws |
| **Private law** | Between private persons | Contract Act; Transfer of Property Act |
| **Municipal (national) law** | Law within one country | All Indian Acts. Parliament's power: **Art. 245–246** and the **Seventh Schedule** |
| **International law** | Between nations | Treaties. Parliament can make laws to implement them: **Art. 253** |
| **Codified law** | Written in an Act | Contract Act, 1872 |
| **Uncodified law** | Developed by courts | Most of the law of torts (negligence, nuisance) |
| **Personal law** | Depends on religion | Hindu Marriage Act, 1955; Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937, **S.2** |

## 3. Sources of Law in India
| Source | Provision that gives it force |
|---|---|
| 1. **Constitution** (supreme) | **Art. 13** (laws against fundamental rights are void); **Art. 245** (extent of laws made by Parliament and States) |
| 2. **Legislation** | **Art. 246** with the **Seventh Schedule**: List I Union, List II State, List III Concurrent. If a central and a state law on a Concurrent subject conflict, the central law generally prevails: **Art. 254** |
| 3. **Delegated legislation** | Power given by the parent Act. Examples: **CPC S.122** (High Courts may amend the Orders); **SEBI Act S.30** (SEBI makes regulations). Included in "law" by **Art. 13(3)(a)** |
| 4. **Judicial precedent** | **Art. 141**: law declared by the Supreme Court binds all courts. **Art. 144**: all authorities must act in aid of the Supreme Court. **Art. 227**: High Court's superintendence over courts in the State |
| 5. **Custom** | **Art. 13(3)(a)**; **Hindu Marriage Act S.3(a)** |
| 6. **Personal law and equity** | Shariat Act **S.2**; Hindu law statutes; "justice, equity and good conscience" where no law applies |

### Precedent: words you must know
- **Ratio decidendi:** the *reason for the decision*. This is the binding part under **Art. 141**.
- **Obiter dicta:** "things said by the way". Only persuasive.
- **Stare decisis:** "stand by what has been decided".

> **Trick:** the **ratio** is the **root**, the **obiter** is the **ornament**.

## 5. The Three Organs of the State
| Organ | Role | Constitutional provisions |
|---|---|---|
| **Legislature** | Makes laws | Parliament: **Art. 79**; State Legislatures: **Art. 168** |
| **Executive** | Implements laws | President: **Art. 52–53**; Council of Ministers: **Art. 74**; Governor: **Art. 153–154** |
| **Judiciary** | Interprets laws, decides disputes | Supreme Court: **Art. 124**; High Courts: **Art. 214**; subordinate courts: **Art. 233–237** |

### How a central law is made
| Step | Provision |
|---|---|
| 1. A **Bill** is introduced in either House | **Art. 107** |
| A **Money Bill** (defined in **Art. 110**) can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha | **Art. 109** |
| 2. Both Houses pass it (a deadlock can go to a **joint sitting**) | **Art. 107–108** |
| 3. **President's assent** makes it an Act | **Art. 111** |
| 4. Published in the **Official Gazette** | — |
| 5. **Commencement:** on the date of assent, unless the Act says otherwise | **GCA S.5** |
| State law: Governor's assent | **Art. 200** |
| **Ordinance** when the legislature is not in session; lapses six weeks after reassembly unless approved | **Art. 123** (President), **Art. 213** (Governor) |
| Effect of **repeal** of an Act on pending rights and cases | **GCA S.6** |

> **Example:** the BNS received the President's assent in December 2023, but **S.1(2)** of the BNS said it would come into force on a date notified by the Central Government. That date was 1 July 2024.

## 8. How to Read a Bare Act
Every Act follows a standard layout:
1. **Long title and Preamble:** the purpose of the Act.
2. **Section 1:** short title, extent and commencement. Example: **CPC S.1**; **BNS S.1**.
3. **Section 2 (usually):** **definitions**. Always read these first. Example: **CPC S.2**; **Contract Act S.2**; **BNSS S.2**.
4. **Main sections**, grouped into Chapters or Parts.
5. **Schedules**. Example: the **CPC First Schedule** contains the 51 Orders (made amendable by High Courts under **S.122**).

### Parts of a section
| Part | How to spot it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Sub-section / clause** | S.2(1), (a), (b) | **CPC S.2(2)** defines "decree" |
| **Proviso** | **"Provided that…"**: an exception or condition | **NI Act S.138 proviso (a)–(c)**: conditions before a cheque bounce becomes an offence |
| **Explanation** | Clarifies the rule | **CPC S.11, Explanation IV**: constructive res judicata |
| **Illustration** | A worked example | **Contract Act S.10–30** and the **BSA** have many |
| **Non obstante clause** | **"Notwithstanding anything contained in…"**: overrides other provisions | **IBC S.238**: the Insolvency Code overrides any inconsistent law |

> **"Shall"** is usually **mandatory** and **"may"** usually **discretionary**. Courts sometimes read them differently depending on the context (Class F5).

## 9. How to Read a Case Citation
**Example 1:** *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala*, **(1973) 4 SCC 225**: year 1973, volume 4, *Supreme Court Cases*, page 225.

**Example 2:** *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India*, **AIR 1978 SC 597**: *All India Reporter*, 1978, Supreme Court, page 597. (Interpreted **Art. 21**: any procedure depriving liberty must be fair, just and reasonable.)

**Neutral citation:** since 2023 the Supreme Court numbers reported judgments like **2023 INSC 123**.

> **Free sources:** India Code (indiacode.nic.in) for bare Acts; Supreme Court and High Court websites; Indian Kanoon.

## 10. Latin Maxims with their Statutory Basis
| Maxim | Meaning | Where it appears in law |
|---|---|---|
| **Ignorantia juris non excusat** | Ignorance of the law is no excuse | Only a **mistake of fact** excuses, not a mistake of law: **BNS S.14 and S.17** (old IPC S.76 and S.79); **Contract Act S.21** |
| **Ubi jus ibi remedium** | Where there is a right, there is a remedy | **Art. 32 and 226** (writs); **CPC S.9** |
| **Audi alteram partem** | Hear the other side | **Art. 14 and 21** (*Maneka Gandhi*); **CPC O.5** (service of summons) |
| **Nemo judex in causa sua** | No one should judge their own case | **Art. 14**; principles of natural justice |
| **Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea** | No guilt without a guilty mind | Words like "intentionally", "knowingly", "dishonestly" in BNS offences; "dishonestly" is defined in **BNS S.2(7)** (old IPC S.24) |
| **Res judicata** | A decided matter cannot be re-litigated | **CPC S.11**; criminal side: **Art. 20(2)** and **BNSS S.337** (old CrPC S.300) |

## 11. One Problem, Three Branches of Law (worked example)
A customer books **Sampradaya Hall** and gives a cheque for the balance of ₹1,50,000. The cheque **bounces**.

| Branch | Applicable sections | Parties | Forum |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Criminal** | "Cheque" defined: **NI Act S.6**. Offence: **S.138**; present the cheque within its validity (3 months), notice within **30 days** of the return memo, **15 days** to pay: **S.138 proviso (a)–(c)**. Complaint within **1 month** after that: **S.142(1)(b)**. Interim compensation up to 20%: **S.143A**. Punishment: up to **2 years**, or fine up to **twice the cheque amount**, or both | You = **complainant**; customer = **accused** | Judicial Magistrate, Nandyal |
| **Civil** | Contract: **Contract Act S.2(h)**; compensation for breach: **S.73**. Suit: **CPC S.26, O.7**; or summary suit: **CPC O.37** | You = **plaintiff**; customer = **defendant** | Civil court by value (**CPC S.6, S.15**) |
| **Consumer** | "Consumer": **CPA S.2(7)**; "deficiency": **S.2(11)**; "complaint": **S.2(6)**; complaint filed under **S.35** | Customer = **complainant**; you = **opposite party** | District Consumer Commission (**S.34**) |

> **Lesson:** one set of facts can give rise to civil, criminal and consumer cases at the same time.
