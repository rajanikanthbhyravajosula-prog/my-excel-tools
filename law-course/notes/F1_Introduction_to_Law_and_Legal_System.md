# Class F1: Introduction to Law and the Indian Legal System
_Phase 0: Foundations  |  Law Refresher Course  |  Start here, as a fresher_

> **What you will learn today:** what law is, the types of law, where law comes from, how a law is made, how the courts are arranged, the everyday legal words, and how to read a bare Act and a case citation. Everything else in the course builds on this class.

## 1. What Is Law?
In simple words, **law is a set of rules made or recognised by the State, which everyone must follow, and which the State enforces through courts and penalties.**

Two classic definitions you should know:

| Jurist | Definition | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| **John Austin** (English) | "Law is the **command of the sovereign**, backed by a **sanction**." | Law = an order from the ruler + a punishment for disobeying |
| **John Salmond** | "Law is the body of principles **recognised and applied by the State in the administration of justice**." | Law = what the courts actually apply |

### Why do we need law?
- **Order:** without rules, the strongest person would win every dispute.
- **Justice:** to give each person what is due to them.
- **Rights:** to protect your life, property and freedom.
- **Certainty:** so that people and businesses can plan. You take an advance for a hall booking because the law will enforce the agreement.

### Law, morality and custom
| | Law | Morality | Custom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made by | The State | Society and conscience | Long practice of a community |
| Enforced by | Courts, police, penalties | Shame, guilt | Social pressure |
| Example | Not paying tax is punishable | Lying to a friend is wrong | Giving gifts at weddings |

> Some rules are both law and morality (do not steal). Some are only morality (help the poor). A custom becomes law when courts recognise it as **ancient, certain, reasonable and continuous**.

## 2. Types (Classification) of Law
### A. Civil law vs Criminal law (the most important division)
| Point | Civil law | Criminal law |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Settle disputes between persons; give a **remedy** | **Punish** wrongs against society |
| Who starts the case | The injured person (**plaintiff** or **petitioner**) | The **State** (through the police and the Public Prosecutor), or a **complainant** |
| The other side is called | **Defendant** or **respondent** | **Accused** |
| Case name looks like | *Ramesh v. Suresh* | *State of Andhra Pradesh v. Suresh* |
| Standard of proof | **Preponderance of probabilities** (more likely than not) | **Beyond reasonable doubt** |
| Result | Decree: money, possession, injunction, declaration | Conviction and sentence (jail, fine), or acquittal |
| Main laws | CPC, Contract Act, Transfer of Property Act | BNS, BNSS, BSA |
| Starts with | A **plaint** (suit) or **petition** | An **FIR** or a private **complaint** |

> **Trick:** "Civil = **compensation**; Criminal = **punishment**." Civil cases are about *my right*; criminal cases are about *society's peace*.

### B. Substantive law vs Procedural law
| Substantive law | Procedural law |
|---|---|
| Creates and defines **rights and duties** | Lays down the **method** to enforce those rights |
| *What* the law is | *How* to go to court |
| Contract Act, BNS, Transfer of Property Act | CPC, BNSS, Limitation Act |

> **Trick:** substantive law is the **destination**; procedural law is the **road map**.

### C. Other classifications
| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Public law** | Between the State and citizens | Constitutional law, criminal law, tax law |
| **Private law** | Between private persons | Contract, property, family law |
| **Municipal (national) law** | Law within one country | All Indian Acts |
| **International law** | Between nations | Treaties, UN conventions |
| **Codified law** | Written in an Act | The Contract Act |
| **Uncodified law** | Developed by courts and custom | Most of the law of torts |
| **Personal law** | Depends on a person's religion | Hindu law, Muslim law |

## 3. Sources of Law in India
1. **Constitution:** the supreme source. Any law against it is void (Article 13).
2. **Legislation (statutes):** laws made by Parliament and State Legislatures. These are the main source today.
3. **Delegated (subordinate) legislation:** rules, regulations and notifications made by the government or bodies such as SEBI and the RBI under the power given by an Act.
4. **Judicial precedent:** decisions of higher courts.
  - **Article 141:** the law declared by the Supreme Court binds all courts in India.
  - A High Court's decisions bind all courts below it in that State.
5. **Custom:** recognised customs, especially in family and property matters.
6. **Personal law and equity:** religious laws in family matters; fairness ("justice, equity and good conscience") where no law exists.

### Precedent: two words you must know
- **Ratio decidendi:** the *reason for the decision*. This is the binding part of a judgment.
- **Obiter dicta:** "things said by the way". Remarks not needed for the decision. They are only persuasive, not binding.
- **Stare decisis:** "stand by what has been decided". Courts follow earlier decisions on the same point.

> **Trick:** the **ratio** is the **root**, the **obiter** is the **ornament**.

## 4. A Short History (just the milestones)
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1726 | Charter creating **Mayor's Courts** in Madras, Bombay and Calcutta: the start of English-style courts |
| 1774 | **Supreme Court at Calcutta** set up under the Regulating Act, 1773 |
| 1834 | First **Law Commission** under Lord **Macaulay**; it drafted the Indian Penal Code |
| 1860–1882 | The great codes: IPC (1860), Evidence Act (1872), Contract Act (1872), Transfer of Property Act (1882) |
| 1861 | **Indian High Courts Act**: High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras |
| 1908 | Present **Code of Civil Procedure** |
| 1937 | **Federal Court** of India |
| 26 Jan 1950 | **Constitution** comes into force; the **Supreme Court of India** begins on 28 Jan 1950 |
| 1 Jan 2019 | **High Court of Andhra Pradesh** begins functioning at Amaravati |
| 1 July 2024 | New criminal laws (BNS, BNSS, BSA) replace the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act |

> India follows the **common law** system inherited from England: judges' decisions are a major source of law, alongside written Acts.

## 5. The Three Organs of the State
| Organ | Role | In India |
|---|---|---|
| **Legislature** | Makes laws | Parliament (Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, President); State Legislatures |
| **Executive** | Implements laws | President or Governor, Council of Ministers, government departments, police |
| **Judiciary** | Interprets laws and decides disputes | Supreme Court, High Courts, subordinate courts |

### How a central law is made (Bill to Act)
1. A **Bill** (a draft law) is introduced in Parliament. A **Money Bill** can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
2. It is debated and passed by **both Houses**.
3. The **President gives assent** (Article 111). The Bill now becomes an **Act**.
4. It is published in the **Official Gazette**.
5. It **comes into force** on the date stated in the Act or notified by the government. This is why the BNS was passed in December 2023 but came into force on 1 July 2024.

> **Ordinance:** when Parliament is not in session, the President (Article 123) or a Governor (Article 213) can issue an ordinance with the force of law. It lapses unless approved within **six weeks** of the legislature reassembling.

## 6. The Courts and Where Cases Go
| Level | Civil side | Criminal side |
|---|---|---|
| Top | **Supreme Court of India** | Supreme Court of India |
| State | **High Court** (Amaravati, for AP) | High Court |
| District | **District Judge** | **Sessions Judge** (serious offences such as murder) |
| Middle | **Senior Civil Judge** | **Chief Judicial Magistrate** |
| Base | **Junior Civil Judge** | **Judicial Magistrate First Class** |

- Which civil court hears a suit depends on the **value of the suit** (pecuniary jurisdiction) and the **place** (territorial jurisdiction).
- **Special forums:** Consumer Commissions (District, State, National), Family Courts, Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT), Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT), NCLT, RERA, Lok Adalats.
- **Writs:** a person whose fundamental or legal rights are violated by the State can go directly to the High Court (Article 226) or the Supreme Court (Article 32).

## 7. Everyday Legal Words (Glossary)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Suit** | A civil case started by filing a plaint |
| **Plaint** | The written statement of the plaintiff's claim that starts a suit |
| **Written statement** | The defendant's written reply to a plaint |
| **Petition** | An application to a court asking for relief (e.g. writ petition, divorce petition) |
| **Plaintiff / Defendant** | The person who files a suit / the person sued |
| **Petitioner / Respondent** | The person who files a petition or appeal / the opposite party |
| **Appellant** | The person who files an appeal |
| **Complainant / Accused** | The person who complains of a crime / the person charged with it |
| **Cause of action** | The set of facts that gives a person the right to sue |
| **Jurisdiction** | The power of a court to hear a particular case |
| **Vakalatnama** | The document by which a client authorises an advocate to act for them |
| **Affidavit** | A written statement of facts sworn before an authorised officer |
| **Summons** | A court's order requiring a person to appear |
| **Warrant** | A court's order to arrest a person or search a place |
| **Bail** | Release of an accused person from custody, on conditions, until trial |
| **Ex parte** | In the absence of the other party |
| **Interim / interlocutory** | Temporary, until the final decision |
| **Injunction** | A court order to do or not do something |
| **Stay** | A court order stopping a proceeding or an order temporarily |
| **Adjournment** | Postponing a hearing to another date |
| **Judgment** | The judge's reasons for the decision |
| **Decree** | The formal final result of a civil suit |
| **Order** | Any decision of a court that is not a decree |
| **Appeal** | Asking a higher court to change a lower court's decision |
| **Cause title** | The heading of a case showing the court, case number and parties |
| **Prayer** | The paragraph at the end of a plaint or petition asking for specific relief |
| **Bare Act** | The text of an Act alone, without commentary |

## 8. How to Read a Bare Act
Every Act follows a standard layout:
1. **Long title and Preamble:** the purpose of the Act.
2. **Section 1:** short title, extent (where it applies) and commencement (when it starts).
3. **Section 2 (usually):** **definitions**. Always read these first.
4. **Main sections:** grouped into Chapters or Parts.
5. **Schedules:** forms, lists, tables at the end. In the CPC, the 51 Orders are in the First Schedule.

### Parts of a section
| Part | How to spot it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Section / sub-section** | S.138, S.2(1) | The main rule |
| **Clause** | (a), (b), (c) | Items within a sub-section |
| **Proviso** | Starts with **"Provided that"** | An **exception or condition** to the main rule |
| **Explanation** | Starts with **"Explanation"** | **Clarifies** the meaning of the rule |
| **Illustration** | Starts with **"Illustration"** | A worked **example** (common in the Contract Act and BSA) |
| **Non obstante clause** | **"Notwithstanding anything contained in…"** | This section **overrides** other provisions |

> **"Shall"** usually means **mandatory**; **"may"** usually means **discretionary**. Courts sometimes read them differently depending on context, and you will learn this in Class F5.

## 9. How to Read a Case Citation
**Example 1:** *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala*, **(1973) 4 SCC 225**
- *Kesavananda Bharati*: the **petitioner** (the person who brought the case)
- **v.**: "versus"
- *State of Kerala*: the **respondent**
- **(1973)**: year of the report
- **4**: volume number
- **SCC**: the law report (*Supreme Court Cases*)
- **225**: the page where the judgment begins

**Example 2:** *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India*, **AIR 1978 SC 597**
- **AIR** = *All India Reporter*; **1978** = year; **SC** = Supreme Court; **597** = page.

**Neutral citation (new):** since 2023 the Supreme Court gives every reported judgment a number such as **2023 INSC 123**. It is free and identifies the judgment without a paid law report.

> **Free sources:** Supreme Court and High Court websites, **India Code** (indiacode.nic.in) for bare Acts, and Indian Kanoon for judgments.

## 10. Latin Maxims to Start With
| Maxim | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ignorantia juris non excusat** | Ignorance of the law is no excuse |
| **Ubi jus ibi remedium** | Where there is a right, there is a remedy |
| **Audi alteram partem** | Hear the other side (natural justice) |
| **Nemo judex in causa sua** | No one should be a judge in their own case |
| **Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea** | An act alone does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty |
| **Res judicata** | A matter already decided cannot be re-litigated |

## 11. One Problem, Three Branches of Law (worked example)
A customer books **Sampradaya Hall** and gives a cheque for the balance of ₹1,50,000. The cheque **bounces**.

| Branch | What you can do | Parties | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Criminal** (NI Act, Section 138) | Send a legal notice within **30 days** of the bank's return memo. If the customer does not pay within **15 days** of receiving it, file a complaint within the **next one month**. Punishment: up to **2 years** in prison, or a fine up to **twice the cheque amount**, or both. | You = **complainant**; customer = **accused** | Judicial Magistrate, Nandyal |
| **Civil** (CPC, Contract Act) | File a **suit for recovery** of ₹1,50,000 with interest, or a **summary suit** under Order XXXVII | You = **plaintiff**; customer = **defendant** | Civil court, depending on value |
| **Consumer** (Consumer Protection Act) | If *you* failed to provide the promised service, the *customer* can complain against you | Customer = **complainant**; you = **opposite party** | District Consumer Commission |

> **Lesson:** one set of facts can give rise to civil, criminal and consumer cases at the same time. A good advocate chooses the best remedy, or a combination of them.
